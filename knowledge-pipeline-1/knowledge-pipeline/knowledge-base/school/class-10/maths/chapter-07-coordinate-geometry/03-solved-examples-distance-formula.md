### Example 1: Triangle Classification
Do the points $(3, 2)$, $(-2, -3)$ and $(2, 3)$ form a triangle? 
**Solution:** Let $P(3, 2)$, $Q(-2, -3)$, and $R(2, 3)$.
$PQ = \sqrt{(3+2)^2 + (2+3)^2} = \sqrt{50} \approx 7.07$
$QR = \sqrt{(-2-2)^2 + (-3-3)^2} = \sqrt{52} \approx 7.21$
$PR = \sqrt{(3-2)^2 + (2-3)^2} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$
Since the sum of any two distances is greater than the third, they form a triangle. Since $PQ^2 + PR^2 = QR^2$, it is a right-angled triangle.

### Example 2: Equidistant Point
Find a point on the y-axis equidistant from $A(6, 5)$ and $B(-4, 3)$.
**Solution:** Let the point be $P(0, y)$.
$(6-0)^2 + (5-y)^2 = (-4-0)^2 + (3-y)^2$
$36 + 25 + y^2 - 10y = 16 + 9 + y^2 - 6y$
$4y = 36 \implies y = 9$. The point is $(0, 9)$.
