The zeroes of a polynomial $p(x)$ are the $x$-coordinates of the points where the graph of $y = p(x)$ intersects the $x$-axis.

- **Linear Polynomial:** The graph of $y = ax + b$ is a straight line that intersects the $x$-axis at exactly one point, $\left(-\frac{b}{a}, 0\right)$. Thus, it has exactly one zero.
- **Quadratic Polynomial:** The graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ is a parabola. It can intersect the $x$-axis at two distinct points, one point (coincident), or no points at all. Therefore, a quadratic polynomial has at most two zeroes.
- **Cubic Polynomial:** A polynomial of degree 3 can have at most three zeroes.

**Remark:** In general, a polynomial $p(x)$ of degree $n$ has at most $n$ zeroes.
