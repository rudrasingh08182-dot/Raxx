Trigonometry has practical applications in measuring heights and distances of objects that are difficult to measure directly. Key concepts include:

- **Line of Sight:** The line drawn from the eye of an observer to the point in the object being viewed.
- **Angle of Elevation:** The angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the object is above the horizontal level (looking up).
- **Angle of Depression:** The angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the object is below the horizontal level (looking down).

To determine the height of an object (like a minar), one typically needs the distance from the base, the angle of elevation, and the observer's height. Trigonometric ratios like $\tan A$ or $\cot A$ are commonly used to relate these dimensions.
