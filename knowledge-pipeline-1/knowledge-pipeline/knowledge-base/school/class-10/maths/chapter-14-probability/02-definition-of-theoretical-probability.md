The theoretical probability of an event $E$, denoted by $P(E)$, is defined as:

$P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes favourable to } E}{\text{Number of all possible outcomes of the experiment}}$

This definition, provided by Pierre Simon Laplace in 1795, assumes that all outcomes of the experiment are equally likely. An event having only one outcome is called an elementary event. The sum of the probabilities of all elementary events of an experiment is always 1.
