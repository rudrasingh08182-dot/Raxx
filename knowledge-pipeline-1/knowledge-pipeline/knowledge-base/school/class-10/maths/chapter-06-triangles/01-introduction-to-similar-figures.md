In geometry, two figures are congruent if they have the same shape and size. However, in this chapter, we focus on similar figures, which have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. All congruent figures are similar, but the converse is not true. For example, all circles are similar, all squares are similar, and all equilateral triangles are similar. The concept of similarity is fundamental to indirect measurements, such as calculating the heights of mountains or distances to celestial objects.
