The substitution method is useful when graphical methods are inconvenient due to non-integral coordinates. The steps are:
1. Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation.
2. Substitute this expression into the second equation to get a single-variable equation.
3. Solve for that variable.
4. Substitute the result back into the expression from Step 1 to find the second variable.

Example: Solve $7x - 15y = 2$ and $x + 2y = 3$.
- From $x + 2y = 3$, we get $x = 3 - 2y$.
- Substitute into the first: $7(3 - 2y) - 15y = 2 \Rightarrow 21 - 14y - 15y = 2 \Rightarrow -29y = -19 \Rightarrow y = \frac{19}{29}$.
- Then, $x = 3 - 2(\frac{19}{29}) = \frac{49}{29}$.
