To find the total surface area (TSA) of a solid formed by combining two or more basic solids, we calculate the sum of the curved surface areas (CSA) of the individual parts that are visible on the surface of the new object. 

For example, if a solid is formed by a cylinder with two hemispheres at its ends, the total surface area is:
$TSA = CSA_{cylinder} + 2 \times CSA_{hemisphere}$

It is important to note that the total surface area of the combined object is not necessarily the sum of the total surface areas of the individual components, as the parts that are joined together are no longer part of the external surface.
