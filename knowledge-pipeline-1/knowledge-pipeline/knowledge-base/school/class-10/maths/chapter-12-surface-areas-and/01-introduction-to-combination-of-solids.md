In our daily life, we frequently encounter objects that are combinations of basic solids like cuboids, cones, cylinders, and spheres. For instance, a truck container is a cylinder with two hemispheres at its ends, and a test tube is a combination of a cylinder and a hemisphere. When we need to calculate the surface area, volume, or capacity of such complex objects, we cannot rely on the formulas for individual basic solids directly. Instead, we must break these objects down into their constituent parts to perform the necessary calculations.
