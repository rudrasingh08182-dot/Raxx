A quadratic polynomial is of the form $ax^2 + bx + c$, where $a \neq 0$. When we equate this polynomial to zero, we get a quadratic equation. These equations are essential for solving various real-life problems, such as calculating dimensions based on area constraints. For example, if a hall has an area of 300 square metres with length $(2x + 1)$ and breadth $x$, the relationship is represented by $2x^2 + x - 300 = 0$.

The history of quadratic equations is rich, with contributions from Babylonians, Greeks, and ancient Indian mathematicians. Brahmagupta (C.E. 598–665) provided an explicit formula for equations of the form $ax^2 + bx = c$, and Sridharacharya (C.E. 1025) later derived the quadratic formula for solving equations by completing the square.
