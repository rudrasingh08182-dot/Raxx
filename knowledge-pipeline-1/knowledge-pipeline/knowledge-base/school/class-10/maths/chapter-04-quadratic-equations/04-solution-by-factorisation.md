A real number $\alpha$ is a root of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ if $a\alpha^2 + b\alpha + c = 0$. The roots of the equation are identical to the zeroes of the corresponding quadratic polynomial. A quadratic equation can have at most two roots.

To find roots by factorisation, we split the middle term such that the equation can be written as a product of two linear factors. For example, to solve $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$:
1. Split $-5x$ into $-2x - 3x$.
2. $2x^2 - 2x - 3x + 3 = 2x(x - 1) - 3(x - 1) = (2x - 3)(x - 1)$.
3. Equating factors to zero: $2x - 3 = 0 \implies x = 3/2$ and $x - 1 = 0 \implies x = 1$.
