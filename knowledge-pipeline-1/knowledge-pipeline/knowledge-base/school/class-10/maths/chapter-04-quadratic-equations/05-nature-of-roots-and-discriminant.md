The roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are given by the quadratic formula:
$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

The term $D = b^2 - 4ac$ is called the discriminant, which determines the nature of the roots:
1. If $b^2 - 4ac > 0$, there are two distinct real roots.
2. If $b^2 - 4ac = 0$, there are two equal real roots: $x = -b/2a$.
3. If $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, there are no real roots.

Example: For $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$, $D = (-4)^2 - 4(2)(3) = 16 - 24 = -8$. Since $D < 0$, there are no real roots.
