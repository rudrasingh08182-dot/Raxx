The position vector of a particle is given by $\vec{r} = 3.0t \hat{i} + 2.0t^2 \hat{j} + 5.0 \hat{k}$, where $t$ is in seconds and the coefficients have the proper units for $\vec{r}$ to be in metres. 

(a) Find $\vec{v}(t)$ and $\vec{a}(t)$ of the particle.
(b) Find the magnitude and direction of $\vec{v}(t)$ at $t = 1.0$ s.

**Answer**

$\vec{v}(t) = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt} (3.0t \hat{i} + 2.0t^2 \hat{j} + 5.0 \hat{k}) = 3.0 \hat{i} + 4.0t \hat{j}$

$\vec{a}(t) = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = 4.0 \hat{j}$

$\vec{a} = 4.0 \text{ m s}^{-2}$ along $y$-direction.

At $t = 1.0$ s, $\vec{v} = 3.0 \hat{i} + 4.0 \hat{j}$.
Its magnitude is $v = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5.0 \text{ m s}^{-1}$ and direction is $\theta = \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{v_y}{v_x} \right) = \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{4}{3} \right) \cong 53^{\circ}$ with $x$-axis.

### 3.8 MOTION IN A PLANE WITH CONSTANT ACCELERATION

Suppose that an object is moving in $x-y$ plane and its acceleration $\vec{a}$ is constant. Over an interval of time, the average acceleration will equal this constant value. Now, let the velocity of the object be $\vec{v}_0$ at time $t = 0$ and $\vec{v}$ at time $t$. Then, by definition:

$\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{v} - \vec{v}_0}{t - 0}$

Or, $\vec{v} = \vec{v}_0 + \vec{a}t$ (3.33a)

In terms of components:
$v_x = v_{ox} + a_x t$
$v_y = v_{oy} + a_y t$ (3.33b)

Let us now find how the position $\vec{r}$ changes with time. We follow the method used in the one-dimensional case. Let $\vec{r}_0$ and $\vec{r}$ be the position vectors of the particle at time $0$ and $t$ and let the velocities at these instants be $\vec{v}_0$ and $\vec{v}$. Then, over this time interval $t$, the average velocity is $(\vec{v}_0 + \vec{v})/2$. The displacement is the average velocity multiplied by the time interval.
