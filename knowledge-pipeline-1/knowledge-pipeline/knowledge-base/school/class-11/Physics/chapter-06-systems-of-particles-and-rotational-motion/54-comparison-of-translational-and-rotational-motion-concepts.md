Its similarity with the corresponding expression $dW = F ds$ for linear (translational) motion is obvious. Dividing both sides of Eq. (6.39) by $dt$ gives:

$P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \tau \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \tau \omega$

or $P = \tau \omega$ (6.40)

This is the instantaneous power. Compare this expression for power in the case of rotational motion about a fixed axis with that of power in the case of linear motion, $P = Fv$.

### Table 6.2 Comparison of Translational and Rotational Motion

| | Linear Motion | Rotational Motion about a Fixed Axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Displacement $x$ | Angular displacement $\theta$ |
| 2 | Velocity $v = dx/dt$ | Angular velocity $\omega = d\theta/dt$ |
| 3 | Acceleration $a = dv/dt$ | Angular acceleration $\alpha = d\omega/dt$ |
| 4 | Mass $M$ | Moment of inertia $I$ |
| 5 | Force $F = Ma$ | Torque $\tau = I \alpha$ |
| 6 | Work $dW = F ds$ | Work $W = \tau d\theta$ |
| 7 | Kinetic energy $K = Mv^2/2$ | Kinetic energy $K = I\omega^2/2$ |
| 8 | Power $P = F v$ | Power $P = \tau \omega$ |
| 9 | Linear momentum $p = Mv$ | Angular momentum $L = I\omega$ |
