Heat conduction may be described quantitatively as the time rate of heat flow in a material for a given temperature difference. Consider a metallic bar of length $L$ and uniform cross-section $A$ with its two ends maintained at different temperatures. This can be done, for example, by putting the ends in thermal contact with large reservoirs at temperatures, say, $T_C$ and $T_D$, respectively (Fig. 10.14). Let us assume the ideal condition that the sides of the bar are fully insulated so that no heat is exchanged between the sides and the surroundings. After sometime, a steady state is reached; the temperature of the bar decreases uniformly with distance from $T_C$ to $T_D$; ($T_C > T_D$).
