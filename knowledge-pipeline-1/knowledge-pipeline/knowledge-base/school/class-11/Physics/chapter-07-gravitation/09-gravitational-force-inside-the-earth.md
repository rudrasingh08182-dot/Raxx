Hence, at a point outside the earth, the gravitational force is just as if its entire mass of the earth is concentrated at its centre. For a point inside the earth, the situation is different. This is illustrated in Fig. 7.7. The mass $m$ is in a mine located at a depth $d$ below the surface of the Earth of mass $M_E$ and radius $R_E$. We treat the Earth to be spherically symmetric. Again consider the earth to be made up of concentric shells as before and a point mass $m$ situated at a distance $r$ from the centre. The point $P$ lies outside the sphere of radius $r$. For the shells of radius greater than $r$, the point $P$ lies inside. Hence according to result stated in the last section, they exert no gravitational force on mass $m$ kept at $P$. The shells with radius $\le r$ make up a sphere of radius $r$ for which the point $P$ lies on the surface. This smaller sphere therefore exerts a force on a mass $m$ at $P$ as if its mass $M_r$ is concentrated at the centre. Thus the force on the mass $m$ at $P$ has a magnitude:

$F = \frac{GM_r m}{r^2}$ (7.9)

We assume that the entire earth is of uniform density and hence its mass is:

$M_E = \frac{4}{3} \pi R_E^3 \rho$

where $M_E$ is the mass of the earth, $R_E$ is its radius and $\rho$ is the density. On the other hand the mass of the sphere $M_r$ of radius $r$ is $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \rho$.
