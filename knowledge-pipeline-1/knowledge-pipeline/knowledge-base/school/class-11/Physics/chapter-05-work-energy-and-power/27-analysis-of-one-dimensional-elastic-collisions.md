The final velocity $v_f$ is given by:
$v_f = v_i + \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} v_i$ (5.22)

The loss in kinetic energy on collision is:
$\Delta K = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_{1i}^2 - \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) v_f^2$

Using Eq. (5.22):
$\Delta K = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_{1i}^2 - \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) \left[ \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} v_i \right]^2$
$\Delta K = \frac{1}{2} \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2} v_i^2$
which is a positive quantity as expected.

Consider next an elastic collision. Using the above nomenclature with $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0$, the momentum and kinetic energy conservation equations are:
$m_1 v_{1i} = m_1 v_{1f} + m_2 v_{2f}$ (5.23)
$\frac{1}{2} m_1 v_{1i}^2 = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_{1f}^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_2 v_{2f}^2$ (5.24)

From Eqs. (5.23) and (5.24) it follows that:
$m_1 (v_{1i}^2 - v_{1f}^2) = m_2 v_{2f}^2$
$m_1 (v_{1i} - v_{1f})(v_{1i} + v_{1f}) = m_2 v_{2f}^2$

Also from Eq. (5.23):
$m_1 (v_{1i} - v_{1f}) = m_2 v_{2f}$

Dividing the two equations:
$v_{1i} + v_{1f} = v_{2f}$
Hence, $v_{2f} = v_{1i} + v_{1f}$ (5.25)

Substituting this in Eq. (5.23), we obtain:
$v_{1f} = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} v_{1i}$ (5.26)
and
$v_{2f} = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2} v_{1i}$ (5.27)

Thus, the ‘unknowns’ { $v_{1f}, v_{2f}$ } are obtained in terms of the ‘knowns’ { $m_1, m_2, v_{1i}$ }. Special cases of our analysis are interesting.

**Case I:** If the two masses are equal ($m_1 = m_2$)
$v_{1f} = 0$
$v_{2f} = v_{1i}$
The first mass comes to rest and pushes off the second mass with its initial speed on collision.

**Case II:** If one mass dominates, e.g., $m_2 \gg m_1$
$v_{1f} \approx -v_{1i}$
$v_{2f} \approx 0$
The heavier mass is undisturbed while the lighter mass reverses its velocity.
