(5.14)
$v_C^2 = gL$

At B, the energy is:
$E_B = \frac{1}{2}mv_B^2 + mgL$

Equating this to the energy at A and employing the result from (i), namely $v_0^2 = 5gL$,

$\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_B^2 + mgL$

$\frac{5}{2}mgL = \frac{1}{2}mv_B^2 + mgL$

$\frac{3}{2}mgL = \frac{1}{2}mv_B^2$

$v_B^2 = 3gL$
