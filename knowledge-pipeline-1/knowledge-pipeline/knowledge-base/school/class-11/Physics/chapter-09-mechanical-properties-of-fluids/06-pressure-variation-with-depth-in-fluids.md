### 9.2.2 Variation of Pressure with Depth

Consider a fluid at rest in a container. In Fig. 9.3 point 1 is at height h above a point 2. The pressures at points 1 and 2 are $P_1$ and $P_2$ respectively. Consider a cylindrical element of fluid having area of base A and height h. As the fluid is at rest the resultant horizontal forces should be zero and the resultant vertical forces should balance the weight of the element. The forces acting in the vertical direction are due to the fluid pressure at the top ($P_1A$) acting downward, at the bottom ($P_2A$) acting upward. If $mg$ is weight of the fluid in the cylinder we have:

$(P_2 - P_1) A = mg$ (9.5)

Now, if $\rho$ is the mass density of the fluid, we have the mass of fluid to be $m = \rho V = \rho hA$ so that:

$P_2 - P_1 = \rho gh$ (9.6)
