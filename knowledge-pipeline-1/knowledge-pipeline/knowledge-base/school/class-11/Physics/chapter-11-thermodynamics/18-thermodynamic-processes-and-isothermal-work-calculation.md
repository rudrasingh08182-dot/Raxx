### 11.8.2 Isothermal process

For an isothermal process ($T$ fixed), the ideal gas equation gives:

$PV = \text{constant}$

i.e., pressure of a given mass of gas varies inversely as its volume. This is nothing but Boyle’s Law. Suppose an ideal gas goes isothermally (at temperature $T$) from its initial state ($P_1, V_1$) to the final state ($P_2, V_2$). At any intermediate stage with pressure $P$ and volume change from $V$ to $V + \Delta V$ ($\Delta V$ small):

$\Delta W = P \Delta V$

Taking ($\Delta V \to 0$) and summing the quantity $\Delta W$ over the entire process,

$W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P dV = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} \frac{\mu RT}{V} dV = \mu RT \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}$ (11.12)
