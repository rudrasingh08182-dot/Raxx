Using the law of equipartition of energy, the total internal energy of a mole of such a gas is $U = \frac{5}{2} k_B T N_A = \frac{5}{2} RT$ (12.32). The molar specific heats are then given by $C_v \text{ (rigid diatomic)} = \frac{5}{2} R$, $C_p = \frac{7}{2} R$ (12.33), $\gamma \text{ (rigid diatomic)} = \frac{7}{5}$ (12.34). If the diatomic molecule is not rigid but has in addition a vibrational mode, $U = \left( \frac{5}{2} k_B T + k_B T \right) N_A = \frac{7}{2} RT$, $C_v = \frac{7}{2} R$, $C_p = \frac{9}{2} R$, $\gamma = \frac{9}{7}$ (12.35).

### 12.6.3 Polyatomic Gases
In general, a polyatomic molecule has 3 translational, 3 rotational degrees of freedom and a certain number ($f$) of vibrational modes. According to the law of equipartition of energy, it is easily seen that one mole of such a gas has $U = (3 k_B T + f k_B T) N_A$, i.e., $C_v = (3 + f) R$, $C_p = (4 + f) R$, $\gamma = \frac{4 + f}{3 + f}$ (12.36).
