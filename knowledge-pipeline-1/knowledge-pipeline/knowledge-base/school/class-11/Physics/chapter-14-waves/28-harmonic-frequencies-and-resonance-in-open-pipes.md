The first harmonic frequency is given by $\nu_1 = \frac{v}{2L} = \frac{v}{\lambda}$ (open pipe), where $L$ is the length of the pipe. The frequency of its $n^{th}$ harmonic is: $\nu_n = \frac{nv}{2L}$, for $n = 1, 2, 3, ...$ (open pipe). First few modes of an open pipe are shown in Fig. 14.15. For $L = 30.0$ cm, $v = 330$ m s⁻¹, $\nu_n = \frac{n(330 \text{ m s}^{-1})}{0.6 \text{ m}} = 550n \text{ s}^{-1}$. Clearly, a source of frequency 1.1 kHz will resonate at $\nu_2$, i.e., the second harmonic. Fundamental or first harmonic, third harmonic, fifth harmonic.
