(14.2), the displacement function for a longitudinal wave is written as, $s(x, t) = a \sin (kx - \omega t + \phi)$ (14.9) where $s(x, t)$ is the displacement of an element of the medium in the direction of propagation of the wave at position $x$ and time $t$. In Eq. (14.9), $a$ is the displacement amplitude; other quantities have the same meaning as in case of a transverse wave except that the displacement function $y(x, t)$ is to be replaced by the function $s(x, t)$. * Here again, ‘radian’ could be dropped and the units could be written merely as $m^{-1}$. Thus, $k$ represents $2\pi$ times the number of waves (or the total phase difference) that can be accommodated per unit length, with SI units $m^{-1}$.
