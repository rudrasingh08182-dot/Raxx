The theoretical probability (also called classical probability) of an event $E$, written as $P(E)$, is defined as:

$P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes favourable to } E}{\text{Number of all possible outcomes of the experiment}}$

where we assume that the outcomes of the experiment are equally likely. We will briefly refer to theoretical probability as probability. This definition of probability was given by Pierre Simon Laplace in 1795. Reprint 2026-27
