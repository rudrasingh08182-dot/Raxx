Considering the example of $NH_4^+$ and $NH_3$, we see:

$NH_4^+(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + NH_3(aq)$
$K_a = \frac{[H_3O^+][NH_3]}{[NH_4^+]} = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$

$NH_3(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)$
$K_b = \frac{[NH_4^+][OH^-]}{[NH_3]} = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$

Net: $2H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)$
$K_w = [H_3O^+][OH^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14} M$

Where, $K_a$ represents the strength of $NH_4^+$ as an acid and $K_b$ represents the strength of $NH_3$ as a base. It can be seen from the net reaction that the equilibrium constant is equal to the product of equilibrium constants $K_a$ and $K_b$ for the reactions added. Thus,

$K_a \times K_b = \left\{ \frac{[H_3O^+][NH_3]}{[NH_4^+]} \right\} \times \left\{ \frac{[NH_4^+][OH^-]}{[NH_3]} \right\}$
$= [H_3O^+][OH^-] = K_w$
$= (5.6 \times 10^{-10}) \times (1.8 \times 10^{-5}) = 1.0 \times 10^{-14} M$
