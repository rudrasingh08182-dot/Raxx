### Hydronium and Hydroxyl ions
Hydrogen ion by itself is a bare proton with very small size (~$10^{-15}$ m radius) and intense electric field, binds itself with the water molecule at one of the two available lone pairs on it giving $H_3O^+$. This species has been detected in many compounds (e.g., $H_3O^+Cl^-$) in the solid state. In aqueous solution the hydronium ion is further hydrated to give species like $H_5O_2^+$, $H_7O_3^+$ and $H_9O_4^+$. Similarly the hydroxyl ion is hydrated to give several ionic species like $H_3O_2^-$, $H_5O_3^-$ and $H_7O_4^-$ etc. The basic solution is formed due to the presence of hydroxyl ions. In this reaction, water molecule acts as proton donor and ammonia molecule acts as proton acceptor and are thus, called Lowry-Brönsted acid and $H_9O_4^+$.
