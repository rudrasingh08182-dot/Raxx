Therefore, $n/V$ is concentration expressed in $\text{mol/m}^3$. If concentration $c$ is in $\text{mol/L}$ or $\text{mol/dm}^3$, and $p$ is in bar then $p = cRT$. We can also write $p = [\text{gas}]RT$. Here, $R = 0.0831 \text{ bar litre/mol K}$. At constant temperature, the pressure of the gas is proportional to its concentration i.e., $p \propto [\text{gas}]$.

For reaction in equilibrium:
$H_2(g) + I_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2HI(g)$

We can write either:
$K_c = \frac{[HI]^2}{[H_2][I_2]}$ or $K_p = \frac{p_{HI}^2}{p_{H_2}p_{I_2}} \quad (6.12)$

Further, since $p_{HI} = [HI]RT$, $p_{H_2} = [H_2]RT$, and $p_{I_2} = [I_2]RT$, therefore,
$K_p = \frac{([HI]RT)^2}{([H_2]RT)([I_2]RT)} = \frac{[HI]^2(RT)^2}{[H_2][I_2](RT)^2} = \frac{[HI]^2}{[H_2][I_2]} = K_c \quad (6.13)$

In this example, $K_p = K_c$ i.e., both equilibrium constants are equal. However, this is not always the case. For example in reaction:
$N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)$
$K_p = \frac{p_{NH_3}^2}{p_{N_2}p_{H_2}^3} = \frac{([NH_3]RT)^2}{([N_2]RT)([H_2]RT)^3} = \frac{[NH_3]^2(RT)^2}{[N_2][H_2]^3(RT)^4} = \frac{[NH_3]^2}{[N_2][H_2]^3}(RT)^{-2} = K_c(RT)^{-2} \quad (6.14)$

Similarly, for a general reaction:
$aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$
$K_p = \frac{p_C^c p_D^d}{p_A^a p_B^b} = \frac{([C]RT)^c ([D]RT)^d}{([A]RT)^a ([B]RT)^b} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b} (RT)^{(c+d)-(a+b)} = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n} \quad (6.15)$

where $\Delta n = (\text{number of moles of gaseous products}) - (\text{number of moles of gaseous reactants})$ in the balanced chemical equation.

It is necessary that while calculating the value of $K_p$, pressure should be expressed in bar because standard state for pressure is 1 bar. We know from Unit 1 that: $1 \text{ pascal, Pa} = 1 \text{ Nm}^{-2}$, and $1 \text{ bar} = 10^5 \text{ Pa}$.
