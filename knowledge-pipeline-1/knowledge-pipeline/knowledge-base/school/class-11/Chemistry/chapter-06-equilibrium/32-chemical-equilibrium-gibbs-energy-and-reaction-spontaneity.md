Let $x$ mol per litre of $PCl_5$ be dissociated.
At equilibrium:

| Species | Concentration |
| :--- | :--- |
| $PCl_5$ | $(3-x)$ |
| $PCl_3$ | $x$ |
| $Cl_2$ | $x$ |

$K_c = \frac{[PCl_3][Cl_2]}{[PCl_5]}$
$1.8 = \frac{x^2}{(3 - x)}$
$x^2 + 1.8x - 5.4 = 0$
$x = \frac{-1.8 \pm \sqrt{(1.8)^2 - 4(-5.4)}}{2}$
$x = \frac{-1.8 \pm \sqrt{3.24 + 21.6}}{2}$
$x = \frac{-1.8 \pm 4.98}{2}$
$x = \frac{-1.8 + 4.98}{2} = 1.59$
$[PCl_5] = 3.0 - x = 3 - 1.59 = 1.41 \text{ M}$
$[PCl_3] = [Cl_2] = x = 1.59 \text{ M}$

Taking antilog of both sides, we get,
$K = e^{-\Delta G^\circ/RT} \quad (6.23)$

Hence, using the equation (6.23), the reaction spontaneity can be interpreted in terms of the value of $\Delta G^\circ$.

* If $\Delta G^\circ < 0$, then $-\Delta G^\circ/RT$ is positive, and $e^{-\Delta G^\circ/RT} > 1$, making $K > 1$, which implies a spontaneous reaction or the reaction which proceeds in the forward direction to such an extent that the products are present predominantly.
* If $\Delta G^\circ > 0$, then $-\Delta G^\circ/RT$ is negative, and $e^{-\Delta G^\circ/RT} < 1$, that is, $K < 1$, which implies a non-spontaneous reaction or a reaction which proceeds in the forward direction to such a small degree that only a very minute quantity of product is formed.

### Problem 6.10
The value of $\Delta G^\circ$ for the phosphorylation of glucose in glycolysis is $13.8 \text{ kJ/mol}$. Find the value of $K_c$ at $298 \text{ K}$.

**Solution**
$\Delta G^\circ = 13.8 \text{ kJ/mol} = 13.8 \times 10^3 \text{ J/mol}$
Also, $\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K_c$
Hence, $\ln K_c = \frac{-13.8 \times 10^3 \text{ J/mol}}{8.314 \text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \times 298 \text{ K}}$
$\ln K_c = -5.569$
$K_c = e^{-5.569}$
$K_c = 3.81 \times 10^{-3}$

### Problem 6.11
Hydrolysis of sucrose gives,
$\text{Sucrose} + H_2O \rightleftharpoons \text{Glucose} + \text{Fructose}$
Equilibrium constant $K_c$ for the reaction is $2 \times 10^{13}$ at $300 \text{ K}$. Calculate $\Delta G^\circ$ at $300 \text{ K}$.

**Solution**
$\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K_c$
$\Delta G^\circ = -8.314 \text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \times 300 \text{ K} \times \ln(2 \times 10^{13})$
$\Delta G^\circ = -7.64 \times 10^4 \text{ J mol}^{-1}$

### 6.7 Relationship Between Equilibrium Constant K, Reaction Quotient Q and Gibbs Energy G
The value of $K_c$ for a reaction does not depend on the rate of the reaction. However, as you have studied in Unit 5, it is directly related to the thermodynamics of the reaction and in particular, to the change in Gibbs energy, $\Delta G$. If,
* $\Delta G$ is negative, then the reaction is spontaneous and proceeds in the forward direction.
