Orbitals with $n$ greater than 3 ($4d$, $5d$...) also have shapes similar to $3d$ orbitals, but differ in energy and size. Besides the radial nodes (i.e., probability density function is zero), the probability density functions for the $np$ and $nd$ orbitals are zero at the plane(s), passing through the nucleus (origin). For example, in case of $p_z$ orbital, $xy$-plane is a nodal plane; in case of $d_{xy}$ orbital, there are two nodal planes passing through the origin and bisecting the $xy$ plane containing $z$-axis. These are called angular nodes and number of angular nodes are given by '$l$', i.e., one angular node for $p$ orbitals, two angular nodes for '$d$' orbitals and so on. The total number of nodes are given by $(n-1)$, i.e., sum of $l$ angular nodes and $(n - l - 1)$ radial nodes.

### 2.6.3 Energies of orbitals
The energy of an electron in a hydrogen atom is determined solely by the principal quantum number.
