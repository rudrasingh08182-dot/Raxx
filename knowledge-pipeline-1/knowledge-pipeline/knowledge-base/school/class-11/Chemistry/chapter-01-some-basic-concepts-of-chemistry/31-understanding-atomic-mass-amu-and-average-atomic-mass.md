And $1 \text{ amu} = 1.66056 \times 10^{-24} \text{ g}$

Mass of an atom of hydrogen $= 1.6736 \times 10^{-24} \text{ g}$

Thus, in terms of amu, the mass of hydrogen atom $= \frac{1.6736 \times 10^{-24} \text{ g}}{1.66056 \times 10^{-24} \text{ g}} = 1.0078 \text{ amu} \approx 1.0080 \text{ amu}$

Similarly, the mass of oxygen-16 ($^{16}\text{O}$) atom would be 15.995 amu.

At present, ‘amu’ has been replaced by ‘u’, which is known as unified mass.

When we use atomic masses of elements in calculations, we actually use average atomic masses of elements, which are explained below.
