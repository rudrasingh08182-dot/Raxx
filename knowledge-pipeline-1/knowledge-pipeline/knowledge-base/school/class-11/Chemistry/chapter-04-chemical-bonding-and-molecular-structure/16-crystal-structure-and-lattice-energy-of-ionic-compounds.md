Ionic compounds in the crystalline state consist of orderly three-dimensional arrangements of cations and anions held together by coulombic interaction energies. These compounds crystallise in different crystal structures determined by the size of the ions, their packing arrangements and other factors. The crystal structure of sodium chloride, NaCl (rock salt), for example is shown below. Rock salt structure.

In ionic solids, the sum of the electron gain enthalpy and the ionization enthalpy may be positive but still the crystal structure gets stabilized due to the energy released in the formation of the crystal lattice. For example: the ionization enthalpy for $Na^+(g)$ formation from $Na(g)$ is $495.8 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$; while the electron gain enthalpy for the change $Cl(g) + e^- \rightarrow Cl^-(g)$ is, $-348.7 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ only. The sum of the two, $147.1 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ is more than compensated for by the enthalpy of lattice formation of $NaCl(s)$ ($-788 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
