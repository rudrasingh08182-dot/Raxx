Net dipole moment of $6.17 \times 10^{-30} \text{ C m}$ ($1 \text{ D} = 3.33564 \times 10^{-30} \text{ C m}$) is the resultant of the dipole moments of two O–H bonds. Net Dipole moment, $\mu = 1.85 \text{ D} = 1.85 \times 3.33564 \times 10^{-30} \text{ C m} = 6.17 \times 10^{-30} \text{ C m}$.

The dipole moment in case of $\text{BeF}_2$ is zero. This is because the two equal bond dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel the effect of each other. In tetra-atomic molecule, for example in $\text{BF}_3$, the dipole moment is zero although the B–F bonds are oriented at an angle of $120^{\circ}$ to one another, the three bond moments give a net sum of zero as the resultant of any two is equal and opposite to the third.
