The change in Gibbs energy for the system, $\Delta G_{sys}$ can be written as $\Delta G_{sys} = \Delta H_{sys} - T\Delta S_{sys}$. At constant temperature, $\Delta G_{sys} = \Delta H_{sys} - T\Delta S_{sys}$. Usually the subscript ‘system’ is dropped and we simply write this equation as $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$ (5.21). Thus, Gibbs energy change = enthalpy change – temperature × entropy change, and is referred to as the Gibbs equation, one of the most important equations in chemistry. Here, we have considered both terms together for spontaneity: energy (in terms of $\Delta H$) and entropy ($\Delta S$, a measure of disorder) as indicated earlier. Dimensionally if we analyse, we find that $\Delta G$ has units of energy because, both $\Delta H$ and the $T\Delta S$ are energy terms, since $T\Delta S = (K) (J/K) = J$. Now let us consider how $\Delta G$ is related to reaction spontaneity.
