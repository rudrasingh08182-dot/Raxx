Note that the system may be defined by physical boundaries, like a beaker or test tube, or the system may simply be defined by a set of Cartesian coordinates specifying a particular volume in space. It is necessary to think of the system as separated from the surroundings by some sort of wall which may be real or imaginary. The wall that separates the system from the surroundings is called the boundary. We could have chosen only the reactants as the system, then the walls of the beakers will act as the boundary.
