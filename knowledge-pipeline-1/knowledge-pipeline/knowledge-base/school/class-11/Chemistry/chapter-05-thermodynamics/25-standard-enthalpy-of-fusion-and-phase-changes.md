Changes. Ice, for example, requires heat for melting. Normally this melting takes place at constant pressure (atmospheric pressure) and during phase change, temperature remains constant (at 273 K).

$H_2O(s) \rightarrow H_2O(l); \Delta_{fus}H^\ominus = 6.00 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Here $\Delta_{fus}H^\ominus$ is enthalpy of fusion in standard state. If water freezes, then process is reversed and equal amount of heat is given off to the surroundings.

The enthalpy change that accompanies melting of one mole of a solid substance in standard state is called standard enthalpy of fusion or molar enthalpy of fusion, $\Delta_{fus}H^\ominus$.

Melting of a solid is endothermic, so all enthalpies of fusion are positive.
