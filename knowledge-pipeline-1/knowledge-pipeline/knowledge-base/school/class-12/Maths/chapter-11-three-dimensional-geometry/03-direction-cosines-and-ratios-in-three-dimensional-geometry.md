For any line, if $a, b, c$ are direction ratios of a line, then $ka, kb, kc; k \neq 0$ is also a set of direction ratios. So, any two sets of direction ratios of a line are also proportional. Also, for any line there are infinitely many sets of direction ratios.

#### 11.2.1 Relation between the direction cosines of a line

Consider a line $RS$ with direction cosines $l, m, n$. Through the origin draw a line parallel to the given line and take a point $P(x, y, z)$ on this line. From $P$ draw a perpendicular $PA$ on the $x$-axis (Fig. 11.2).

Let $OP = r$. Then $\cos \alpha = \frac{OA}{OP} = \frac{x}{r}$. This gives $x = lr$.

Similarly, $y = mr$ and $z = nr$.

Thus $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = r^2 (l^2 + m^2 + n^2)$.

But $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = r^2$.

Hence $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$.
