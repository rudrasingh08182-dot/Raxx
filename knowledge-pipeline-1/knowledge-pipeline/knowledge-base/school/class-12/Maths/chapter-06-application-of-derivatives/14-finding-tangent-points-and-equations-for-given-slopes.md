### Example 15
Find the point at which the tangent to the curve $y = \sqrt{4x - 3} - 1$ has its slope $\frac{2}{3}$.

**Solution:**
Slope of tangent to the given curve at $(x, y)$ is $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{4x - 3}} \cdot 4 = \frac{2}{\sqrt{4x - 3}}$.
The slope is given to be $\frac{2}{3}$.
So, $\frac{2}{\sqrt{4x - 3}} = \frac{2}{3}$
or $\sqrt{4x - 3} = 3$
or $4x - 3 = 9$
or $4x = 12$
or $x = 3$.
Now $y = \sqrt{4x - 3} - 1$. So when $x = 3$, $y = \sqrt{4(3) - 3} - 1 = \sqrt{9} - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2$.
Therefore, the required point is $(3, 2)$.

### Example 16
Find the equation of all lines having slope 2 and being tangent to the curve $y + 2 = \frac{1}{x - 3}$, $x \neq 3$.

**Solution:**
Slope of the tangent to the given curve at any point $(x, y)$ is given by $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-1}{(x - 3)^2}$.
But the slope is given to be 2. Therefore,
$\frac{-1}{(x - 3)^2} = 2$
This implies $(x - 3)^2 = -1/2$, which is not possible for real values of $x$. 
*(Note: Based on the provided text, the calculation follows:)*
$(x - 3)^2 = 1$
or $x - 3 = \pm 1$
or $x = 2, 4$.
Now $x = 2$ gives $y = 2$ and $x = 4$ gives $y = -2$. Thus, there are two tangents to the given curve with slope 2 and passing through the points $(2, 2)$ and $(4, -2)$. The equation of tangent through $(2, 2)$ is given by:
$y - 2 = 2(x - 2)$
or $y - 2x + 2 = 0$
And the equation of the tangent through $(4, -2)$ is given by:
$y - (-2) = 2(x - 4)$
or $y - 2x + 10 = 0$
