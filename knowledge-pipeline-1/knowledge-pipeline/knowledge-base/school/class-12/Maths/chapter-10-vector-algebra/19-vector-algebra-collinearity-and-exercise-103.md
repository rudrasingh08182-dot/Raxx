Example 21: Show that the points $A(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k})$, $B(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 5\hat{k})$ and $C(7\hat{i} - \hat{k})$ are collinear.

Solution: We have
$\vec{AB} = (2-1)\hat{i} + (3-2)\hat{j} + (5-3)\hat{k} = \hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$
$\vec{BC} = (7-2)\hat{i} + (0-3)\hat{j} + (-1-5)\hat{k} = 5\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} - 6\hat{k}$
$\vec{AC} = (7-1)\hat{i} + (0-2)\hat{j} + (-1-3)\hat{k} = 6\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$

Therefore, $\vec{AB} + \vec{BC} = \vec{AC}$.
Hence the points A, B and C are collinear.

Note: In Example 21, one may note that although $\vec{AB} + \vec{BC} = \vec{AC}$, but the points A, B and C do not form the vertices of a triangle.
