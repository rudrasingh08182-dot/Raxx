**Observations**
1. If $\hat{p}$ is the unit vector along a line $l$, then the projection of a vector $\vec{a}$ on the line $l$ is given by $\vec{a} \cdot \hat{p}$.
2. Projection of a vector $\vec{a}$ on other vector $\vec{b}$, is given by $\vec{a} \cdot \hat{b}$ or $\frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}$.
3. If $\theta = 0$, then the projection vector of $\vec{a}$ will be $\vec{a}$ itself and if $\theta = \pi$, then the projection vector of $\vec{a}$ will be $-\vec{a}$.
4. If $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ or $\theta = \frac{3\pi}{2}$, then the projection vector of $\vec{a}$ will be zero vector.

**Remark**
If $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ are the direction angles of vector $\vec{a} = a_1\hat{i} + a_2\hat{j} + a_3\hat{k}$, then its direction cosines may be given as $\cos \alpha, \cos \beta, \cos \gamma$.

Also, note that $a_1, a_2, a_3$ are respectively the projections of $\vec{a}$ along OX, OY and OZ. i.e., the scalar components $a_1, a_2$ and $a_3$ of the vector $\vec{a}$, are precisely the projections of $\vec{a}$ along x-axis, y-axis and z-axis, respectively. Further, if $\vec{a}$ is a unit vector, then it may be expressed in terms of its direction cosines as $\vec{a} = \cos \alpha \hat{i} + \cos \beta \hat{j} + \cos \gamma \hat{k}$.

**Example 13**
Find the angle between two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ with magnitudes 1 and 2 respectively and $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1$.

**Solution**
Given $|\vec{a}|=1, |\vec{b}|=2$ and $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1$. We have $\cos \theta = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|} = \frac{1}{1 \times 2} = \frac{1}{2}$. Thus $\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$.
