Sexual reproduction involves the formation and fusion of male and female gametes, usually from two parents of opposite sex. It is a complex and slow process that creates genetic variation. All sexually reproducing organisms undergo three phases: the juvenile (or vegetative) phase, the reproductive phase, and the senescent (old age) phase. In placental mammals, the reproductive phase involves cyclical changes: the oestrus cycle in non-primates and the menstrual cycle in primates. Sexual reproduction events are categorized into three stages: pre-fertilisation, fertilisation, and post-fertilisation.
