Asexual reproduction involves a single parent producing offspring without the fusion of gametes. The resulting offspring are genetically and morphologically identical to the parent and to each other; such individuals are called clones. This method is common in single-celled organisms, simple plants, and animals. In organisms like Protists and Monerans, cell division (mitosis) is the mode of reproduction. Common asexual structures include zoospores (algae), conidia (Penicillium), buds (Hydra), and gemmules (sponge). In plants, vegetative propagation uses structures like runners, rhizomes, suckers, tubers, offsets, and bulbs to produce new offspring.
