In the presence of an external field $B_0$, which is strong enough, and at low temperatures, the individual atomic dipole moment can be made to align and point in the same direction as $B_0$. Figure 5.7(b) shows a bar of paramagnetic material placed in an external field. The field lines get concentrated inside the material, and the field inside is enhanced. In most cases, this enhancement is slight, being one part in $10^5$. When placed in a non-uniform magnetic field, the bar will tend to move from weak field to strong. Some paramagnetic materials are aluminium, sodium, calcium, oxygen (at STP) and copper chloride. For a paramagnetic material both $\chi$ and $\mu_r$ depend not only on the material, but also (in a simple fashion) on the sample temperature. As the field is increased or the temperature is lowered, the magnetisation increases until it reaches the saturation value at which point all the dipoles are perfectly aligned with the field.
