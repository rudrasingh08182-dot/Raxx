Electric flux $\Delta\phi$ through an area element $\Delta S$ is defined by $\Delta\phi = \mathbf{E} \cdot \Delta\mathbf{S} = E \Delta S \cos\theta$ (1.11), which, as seen before, is proportional to the number of field lines cutting the area element. The angle $\theta$ here is the angle between $\mathbf{E}$ and $\Delta\mathbf{S}$. For a closed surface, with the convention stated already, $\theta$ is the angle between $\mathbf{E}$ and the outward normal $\hat{n}$ to the area element. Notice we could look at the expression $E \Delta S \cos\theta$ in two ways: $E (\Delta S \cos\theta)$ i.e., $E$ times the projection of area element normal to $\mathbf{E}$, or $\Delta S (E \cos\theta)$ i.e., $\Delta S$ times the component of $\mathbf{E}$ normal to the area.

* It will not be proper to say that the number of field lines is equal to $E \Delta S$. The number of field lines is after all, a matter of how many field lines we choose to draw. What is physically significant is the relative number of field lines crossing a given area at different points.
