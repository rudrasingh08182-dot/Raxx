The electric field at any general point P is obtained by adding the electric fields $\vec{E}_{-q}$ due to the charge $-q$ and $\vec{E}_{+q}$ due to the charge $+q$, by the parallelogram law of vectors.

(i) For points on the axis
Let the point P be at distance $r$ from the centre of the dipole on the side of the charge $+q$, as shown in Fig. 1.17(a). Then

$\vec{E}_{-q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q}{(r+a)^2} \hat{p}$ [1.13(a)]

where $\hat{p}$ is the unit vector along the dipole axis (from $-q$ to $+q$). Also

$\vec{E}_{+q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q}{(r-a)^2} \hat{p}$ [1.13(b)]
