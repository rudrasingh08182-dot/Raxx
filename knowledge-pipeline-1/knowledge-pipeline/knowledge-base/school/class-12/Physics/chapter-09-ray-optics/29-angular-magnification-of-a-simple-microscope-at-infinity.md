We have the angle subtended by the image $\theta' = \frac{h'}{v} = \frac{h}{v} \cdot \frac{v}{u} = \frac{h}{-u}$. The angle subtended by the object, when it is at $u = -f$, is $\theta = \frac{h}{f}$ (9.41), as is clear from Fig. 9.23(c). The angular magnification is, therefore, $m = \frac{\theta'}{\theta} = \frac{D}{f}$ (9.42). This is one less than the magnification when the image is at the near point, Eq. (9.39), but the viewing is more comfortable and the difference in magnification is usually small. In subsequent discussions of optical instruments (microscope and telescope) we shall assume the image to be at infinity.
