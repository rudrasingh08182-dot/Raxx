Vapour pressure of chloroform ($CHCl_3$) and dichloromethane ($CH_2Cl_2$) at 298 K are 200 mm Hg and 415 mm Hg respectively. (i) Calculate the vapour pressure of the solution prepared by mixing 25.5 g of $CHCl_3$ and 40 g of $CH_2Cl_2$ at 298 K and, (ii) mole fractions of each component in vapour phase.

(i) Molar mass of $CH_2Cl_2 = 12 \times 1 + 1 \times 2 + 35.5 \times 2 = 85 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$
Molar mass of $CHCl_3 = 12 \times 1 + 1 \times 1 + 35.5 \times 3 = 119.5 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$
Moles of $CH_2Cl_2 = 40 \text{ g} / 85 \text{ g mol}^{-1} = 0.47 \text{ mol}$
Moles of $CHCl_3 = 25.5 \text{ g} / 119.5 \text{ g mol}^{-1} = 0.213 \text{ mol}$
Total number of moles = 0.47 + 0.213 = 0.683 mol
$x_{CH_2Cl_2} = 0.47 \text{ mol} / 0.683 \text{ mol} = 0.688$
$x_{CHCl_3} = 1.00 - 0.688 = 0.312$
Using equation (2.16),
$p_{total} = p_1^0 + (p_2^0 - p_1^0) x_2 = 200 + (415 - 200) \times 0.688$
$= 200 + 147.9 = 347.9 \text{ mm Hg}$

(ii) Using the relation (2.19), $y_i = p_i / p_{total}$, we can calculate the mole fraction of the components in gas phase ($y_i$).
$p_{CH_2Cl_2} = 0.688 \times 415 \text{ mm Hg} = 285.5 \text{ mm Hg}$
$p_{CHCl_3} = 0.312 \times 200 \text{ mm Hg} = 62.4 \text{ mm Hg}$
$y_{CH_2Cl_2} = 285.5 \text{ mm Hg} / 347.9 \text{ mm Hg} = 0.82$
$y_{CHCl_3} = 62.4 \text{ mm Hg} / 347.9 \text{ mm Hg} = 0.18$

Note: Since, $CH_2Cl_2$ is a more volatile component than $CHCl_3$, [$p_{CH_2Cl_2}^0 = 415 \text{ mm Hg}$ and $p_{CHCl_3}^0 = 200 \text{ mm Hg}$] and the vapour phase is also richer in $CH_2Cl_2$ [$y_{CH_2Cl_2} = 0.82$ and $y_{CHCl_3} = 0.18$], it may thus be concluded that at equilibrium, vapour phase will be always rich in the component which is more volatile.
