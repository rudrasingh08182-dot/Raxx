In crystals of FeO, some $Fe^{2+}$ cations are missing and the loss of positive charge is made up by the presence of the required number of $Fe^{3+}$ ions. Solids exhibit an amazing range of electrical conductivities, extending over 27 orders of magnitude ranging from $10^{-20}$ to $10^{7}$ $\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{m}^{-1}$. Solids can be classified into three types on the basis of their conductivities.

(i) Conductors: The solids with conductivities ranging between $10^{4}$ to $10^{7}$ $\text{ohm}^{-1}\text{m}^{-1}$ are called conductors. Metals have conductivities in the order of $10^{7}$ $\text{ohm}^{-1}\text{m}^{-1}$ and are good conductors.
