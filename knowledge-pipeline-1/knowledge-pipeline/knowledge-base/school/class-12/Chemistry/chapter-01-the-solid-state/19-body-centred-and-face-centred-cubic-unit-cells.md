### 1.5.2 Body-Centred Cubic Unit Cell
A body-centred cubic (bcc) unit cell has an atom at each of its corners and also one atom at its body centre. Fig. 1.14 depicts (a) open structure (b) space-filling structure (c) actual portions of atoms belonging to one unit cell. It can be seen that the atom at the body centre wholly belongs to the unit cell in which it is present. Thus, in a body-centred cubic (bcc) unit cell:

(i) 8 corners × $\frac{1}{8}$ per corner atom = $8 \times \frac{1}{8} = 1$ atom
(ii) 1 body centre atom = $1 \times 1 = 1$ atom
∴ Total number of atoms per unit cell = 2 atoms

### 1.5.3 Face-Centred Cubic Unit Cell
A face-centred cubic (fcc) unit cell contains atoms at all the corners and at the centre of all the faces of the cube. It can be seen in Fig. 1.15 that each atom located at the face-centre is shared between two adjacent unit cells and only $\frac{1}{2}$ of each atom belongs to a unit cell. Fig. 1.16 depicts (a) open structure (b) space-filling model and (c) the unit cell with portions of atoms actually belonging to it. Thus, in a face-centred cubic (fcc) unit cell:

(i) 8 corners atoms × $\frac{1}{8}$ atom per unit cell = $8 \times \frac{1}{8} = 1$ atom
(ii) 6 face-centred atoms × $\frac{1}{2}$ atom per unit cell = $6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3$ atoms
∴ Total number of atoms per unit cell = 4 atoms
