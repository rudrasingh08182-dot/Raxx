### Importance and Applications of Coordination Compounds

| Metal Carbonyl | Structure | Coordination Number |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $Ni(CO)_4$ | Tetrahedral | 4 |
| $Fe(CO)_5$ | Trigonal bipyramidal | 5 |
| $Cr(CO)_6$ | Octahedral | 6 |
| $[Mn_2(CO)_{10}]$ | - | - |
| $[Co_2(CO)_8]$ | - | - |

**Fig. 9.13:** Structures of some representative homoleptic metal carbonyls.

The metal-carbon bond in metal carbonyls possesses both $\sigma$ and $\pi$ character. The M–C $\sigma$ bond is formed by the donation of a lone pair of electrons on the carbonyl carbon into a vacant orbital of the metal. The M–C $\pi$ bond is formed by the donation of a pair of electrons from a filled d orbital of metal into the vacant antibonding $\pi^*$ orbital.
