It is not really surprising, therefore, high concentrations of ozone can be dangerously explosive. Due to the ease with which it liberates atoms of nascent oxygen ($O_3 \rightarrow O_2 + O$), it acts as a powerful oxidising agent. For example, it oxidises lead sulphide to lead sulphate and iodide ions to iodine.

$PbS(s) + 4O_3(g) \rightarrow PbSO_4(s) + 4O_2(g)$

$2I^-(aq) + H_2O(l) + O_3(g) \rightarrow 2OH^-(aq) + I_2(s) + O_2(g)$

When ozone reacts with an excess of potassium iodide solution buffered with a borate buffer (pH 9.2), iodine is liberated which can be titrated against a standard solution of sodium thiosulphate. This is a quantitative method for estimating $O_3$ gas.
