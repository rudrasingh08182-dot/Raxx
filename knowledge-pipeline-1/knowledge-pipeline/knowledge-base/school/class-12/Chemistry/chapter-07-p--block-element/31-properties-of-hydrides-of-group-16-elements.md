All the hydrides except water possess reducing property and this character increases from $H_2S$ to $H_2Te$.

| Property | $H_2O$ | $H_2S$ | $H_2Se$ | $H_2Te$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| m.p/K | 273 | 188 | 208 | 222 |
| b.p/K | 373 | 213 | 232 | 269 |
| H–E distance/pm | 96 | 134 | 146 | 169 |
| HEH angle (°) | 104 | 92 | 91 | 90 |
| $\Delta_f H/kJ mol^{-1}$ | –286 | –20 | 73 | 100 |
| $\Delta_{diss} H (H–E) /kJ mol^{-1}$ | 463 | 347 | 276 | 238 |
| Dissociation constant $K_a$ | $1.8 \times 10^{-16}$ | $1.3 \times 10^{-7}$ | $1.3 \times 10^{-4}$ | $2.3 \times 10^{-3}$ |

Table 7.7: Properties of Hydrides of Group 16 Elements

$^a$ Aqueous solution, 298 K
