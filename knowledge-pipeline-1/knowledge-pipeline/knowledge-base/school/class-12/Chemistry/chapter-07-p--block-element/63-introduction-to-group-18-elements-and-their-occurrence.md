Group 18 consists of elements: helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon and oganesson. All these are gases and chemically unreactive. They form very few compounds, because of this they are termed as noble gases.

All these gases except radon and oganesson occur in the atmosphere. Their atmospheric abundance in dry air is ~ 1% by volume of which argon is the major constituent. Helium and sometimes neon are found in minerals of radioactive origin e.g., pitchblende, monazite, cleveite. The main commercial source of helium is natural gas. Xenon and radon are the rarest elements of the group. Radon is obtained as a decay product of $^{226}_{88}\text{Ra}$.

$^{226}_{88}\text{Ra} \rightarrow ^{222}_{86}\text{Rn} + ^{4}_{2}\text{He}$

Oganesson has been synthetically produced by collision of $^{249}_{98}\text{Cf}$ atoms and $^{48}_{20}\text{Ca}$ ions:

$^{249}_{98}\text{Cf} + ^{48}_{20}\text{Ca} \rightarrow ^{294}_{118}\text{Og} + 3^{1}_{0}\text{n}$

### 7.23 Group 18 Elements

#### 7.23.1 Occurrence

Why are the elements of Group 18 known as noble gases?

The elements present in Group 18 have their valence shell orbitals completely filled and, therefore, react with a few elements only under certain conditions. Therefore, they are now known as noble gases.
