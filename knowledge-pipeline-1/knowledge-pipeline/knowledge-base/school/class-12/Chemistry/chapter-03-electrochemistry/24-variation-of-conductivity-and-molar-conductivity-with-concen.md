**Example 3.5**

The electrical resistance of a column of 0.05 mol L⁻¹ NaOH solution of diameter 1 cm and length 50 cm is 5.55 × 10³ ohm. Calculate its resistivity, conductivity and molar conductivity.

**Solution**

$A = \pi r^2 = 3.14 \times (0.5)^2 \text{ cm}^2 = 0.785 \text{ cm}^2 = 0.785 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2$

$l = 50 \text{ cm} = 0.5 \text{ m}$

$\rho = \frac{RA}{l} = \frac{5.55 \times 10^3 \Omega \times 0.785 \text{ cm}^2}{50 \text{ cm}} = 87.135 \Omega \text{ cm}$

$\text{Conductivity } \kappa = \frac{1}{\rho} = \frac{1}{87.135} \text{ S cm}^{-1} = 0.01148 \text{ S cm}^{-1}$

$\text{Molar conductivity, } \Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{c} = \frac{0.01148 \text{ S cm}^{-1} \times 1000 \text{ cm}^3 \text{ L}^{-1}}{0.05 \text{ mol L}^{-1}} = 229.6 \text{ S cm}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$

If we want to calculate the values of different quantities in terms of ‘m’ instead of ‘cm’:

$\rho = \frac{RA}{l} = \frac{5.55 \times 10^3 \Omega \times 0.785 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2}{0.5 \text{ m}} = 87.135 \times 10^{-2} \Omega \text{ m}$

$\kappa = \frac{1}{\rho} = \frac{1}{87.135 \times 10^{-2}} \text{ S m}^{-1} = 1.148 \text{ S m}^{-1}$

$\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa}{c} = \frac{1.148 \text{ S m}^{-1}}{50 \text{ mol m}^{-3}} = 229.6 \times 10^{-4} \text{ S m}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$

**3.4.2 Variation of Conductivity and Molar Conductivity with Concentration**

Both conductivity and molar conductivity change with the concentration of the electrolyte. Conductivity always decreases with decrease in concentration, for both weak and strong electrolytes. This can be explained by the fact that the number of ions per unit volume that carry the current in a solution decreases on dilution. The conductivity of a solution at any given concentration is the conductance of one unit volume of solution kept between two electrodes.
