Nernst showed that for the electrode reaction:
$M^{n+}(aq) + ne^- \rightarrow M(s)$

the electrode potential at any concentration measured with respect to standard hydrogen electrode can be represented by:
$E_{(M^{n+}/M)} = E^{\ominus}_{(M^{n+}/M)} - \frac{RT}{nF} \ln \frac{[M]}{[M^{n+}]}$

But concentration of solid M is taken as unity and we have:
$E_{(M^{n+}/M)} = E^{\ominus}_{(M^{n+}/M)} - \frac{RT}{nF} \ln \frac{1}{[M^{n+}]}$ (3.8)

$E^{\ominus}_{(M^{n+}/M)}$ has already been defined, R is gas constant (8.314 $JK^{-1} mol^{-1}$), F is Faraday constant (96487 $C mol^{-1}$), T is temperature in kelvin and $[M^{n+}]$ is the concentration of the species, $M^{n+}$.
