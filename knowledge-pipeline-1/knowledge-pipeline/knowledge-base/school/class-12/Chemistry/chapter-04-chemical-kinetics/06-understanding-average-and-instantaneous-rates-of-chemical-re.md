It can be seen (Table 4.1) that the average rate falls from $1.90 \times 10^{-4} \text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{s}^{-1}$ to $0.4 \times 10^{-4} \text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{s}^{-1}$. However, average rate cannot be used to predict the rate of a reaction at a particular instant as it would be constant for the time interval for which it is calculated. So, to express the rate at a particular moment of time we determine the instantaneous rate. It is obtained when we consider the average rate at the smallest time interval say $dt$ (i.e., when $\Delta t$ approaches zero). Hence, mathematically for an infinitesimally small $dt$, instantaneous rate is given by:

$r_{av} = -\frac{\Delta [R]}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta [P]}{\Delta t}$ (4.3)

As $\Delta t \to 0$ or $r_{inst} = -\frac{d[R]}{dt} = \frac{d[P]}{dt}$
