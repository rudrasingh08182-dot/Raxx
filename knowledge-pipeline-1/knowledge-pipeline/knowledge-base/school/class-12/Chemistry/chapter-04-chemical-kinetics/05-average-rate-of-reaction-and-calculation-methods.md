97 Chemical Kinetics

Rate of appearance of P = $\frac{\Delta[P]}{\Delta t}$ (4.2)

Since, $\Delta[R]$ is a negative quantity (as concentration of reactants is decreasing), it is multiplied with –1 to make the rate of the reaction a positive quantity.

Equations (4.1) and (4.2) given above represent the average rate of a reaction, $r_{av}$.

Average rate depends upon the change in concentration of reactants or products and the time taken for that change to occur (Fig. 4.1).

Fig. 4.1: Instantaneous and average rate of a reaction

Units of rate of a reaction
From equations (4.1) and (4.2), it is clear that units of rate are concentration time⁻¹. For example, if concentration is in $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ and time is in seconds then the units will be $\text{mol L}^{-1}\text{s}^{-1}$. However, in gaseous reactions, when the concentration of gases is expressed in terms of their partial pressures, then the units of the rate equation will be $\text{atm s}^{-1}$.
